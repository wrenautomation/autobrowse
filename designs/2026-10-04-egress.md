# Egress: where each browser leaves from (2026-10-04)

## Answer first

- Every local browser profile goes out through a named exit. No entry = the machine's own line (`desk`, the Mac's home IP).
- `EGRESS_SITES=web:mobile,linkedin@research:isp` maps a site (`x`: all its profiles) or one profile to an exit.
- An exit is a proxy URL: `EGRESS_<NAME>=http://user:pass@host:port`. Optional `EGRESS_<NAME>_TZ` (the exit's city), `EGRESS_<NAME>_ROTATE` (a URL that gives the exit a new IP), `EGRESS_<NAME>_GAP` (seconds between rotations, default 120).
- On Google's `/sorry` page, a profile behind a rotating exit asks for a new IP and reloads, up to twice. Only then the captcha solver, then a person.
- A rotating exit may not serve a site we sign in to. A signed-in session that hops IPs is its own flag. Startup refuses that config.
- Replaces `BROWSER_PROXY`, `BROWSER_PROXY_SITES`, `BROWSER_PROXY_TIMEZONE` (one exit only, no rotation). None were set.

## Why

William, 2026-10-04: in house, cheapest, extends as we scale; one proxy serves Google and more.

- Google caps exist to protect the home IP (300/day on `web`, 200 for wren's profiles stage, daytime only).
- Datacenter IPs (VPS, EC2) get `/sorry` from Google and the authwall from LinkedIn at once. Useless as exits.
- Residential proxies bill $1.5-8/GB. A Google page is ~0.5-1 MB with assets: $1-8 per 1k searches.
- A 4G modem with a SIM on a Pi (or the Mac) is ~$30-60/mo flat with unlimited data, and rotates its IP on demand (carrier NAT: thousands of real people share it, so Google rarely blocks it). Worth it past ~1k searches/day or ~$50/mo of metered spend.
- A static ISP IP (~$3-5/mo) fits signed-in accounts on a box: one stable IP per account.

So the seam takes any mix: desk today, a 4G exit for signed-out reads, a static exit per signed-in account.

## Not in this

- HTTP (non-browser) calls through an exit. No caller needs one yet: LinkedIn reads stay on Exa's cache (ToS), Google is a browser leg. Add an undici `ProxyAgent` per exit when one does.
- Buying the modem, SIM or proxy: William's call (money).
- Raising Google caps. Once a rotating exit serves `web`, raise the `web` cap and wren's `GOOGLE_PER_DAY` together.

## Decision log

- Rotate before the captcha: a fresh carrier IP passes for free; the solver costs time and sometimes a person.
- Rotation by URL: 4G modem stacks (Proxidize, DIY 3proxy + modem API) and rented mobile proxies all expose a "change IP" link. One shape fits both.
- Exits are operator tools (`EGRESS_` prefix): every owner's browsers use the operator's lines.
